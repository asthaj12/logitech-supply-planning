Key Insight

Supply cuts stop the bleeding but don't clear aging inventory. When Gaming fell 17% post-COVID, the question was: how much of the excess is recoverable through channel action vs. how much becomes true write-off? Separating those two piles in the weekly S&OP — and forcing GTM commercial to own the recovery actions — cleared the backlog in 6–8 weeks vs. 6–9 months via factory cuts alone.

Operating Principle

Status meetings feel productive but produce no decisions. Every S&OP agenda item was required to have one fact base, one owner, and one due date — or it didn't make the agenda. ODMs submitted capacity constraints on a fixed template; deviations were auto-flagged. The result: meeting time on decisions went from 20% to 85%, action-item completion went from 55% to 89%.

Why the Integration Mattered

Launch fill, E&O, and expedite cost had never been in the same room at the same time. When fill rate dropped, expedite spend went up, and E&O went up — but the root cause wasn't visible because the metrics lived in separate reports. Connecting all three in one shared view forced the root cause question in the same meeting where it could be answered.

01
Launch Fill → Inventory at Risk

A fill rate miss on a new product launch doesn't just lose the launch window — it triggers emergency replenishment that arrives after the demand peak, converting fill inventory into E&O. Visible in the same view, the connection drove pre-launch safety stock decisions instead of post-launch expedites.

02
E&O → Margin Erosion Timeline

E&O isn't a static write-off risk — it erodes margin progressively: first as carrying cost, then as promotional concession, then as markdown. Showing the timeline in the S&OP forced earlier GTM action when commercial levers (pull-forwards, bundles) were still at favorable margin.

03
Expedite Cost → When Reallocation is Cheaper

Expedite decisions were previously made in isolation by Sales ("we need it"). Showing expedite cost vs. margin protected vs. regional reallocation cost in one view let the S&OP choose the cheapest path to the same service outcome — saving ~$200K without a single service level miss.
